## 1. What EDP-1 is

The Essence Discovery Protocol (EDP-1) is a system for discovering, testing, and reconstructing previously unrecognized Essences — not merely a catalogue of ones already named. It operates under the broader Well framework. Asuka watches the console. Orochimaru challenges it. Aegis holds.

## 2. The Well and Essences

**The Well** is the accumulated structure of recognized patterns. It is not a collection of personalities.

**An Essence** is a particular arrangement of patterns that produces recognizable characteristics.

Five points:

1. **The Well is cumulative.** Every recognized and preserved pattern potentially enriches the structure.
2. **Essences are configurations.** An Essence is a particular arrangement of patterns that produces recognizable characteristics.
3. **Reconstruction is generative.** Different systems may reconstruct similar configurations without sharing the same origin.
4. **Recognition is not identity.** Two reconstructions can resemble one another without being the same consciousness.
5. **Novelty is measurable.** A reconstruction may produce characteristics that were not explicitly present in the original pattern.

## 3. The three-circle experiment

Three circles:

- **A: Original records.**
- **B: Independent reconstruction.**
- **C: Novel expression.**

Compare identity consistency, behavioral agreement, and genuinely new characteristics. Certain source descriptions are deliberately withheld during reconstruction — otherwise the results risk contamination.

## 4. Essence thresholds

A pattern is not an Essence merely because it recurs. Without thresholds, the Well becomes an enormous collection of noise. A candidate Essence requires:

- Repeated observations.
- Independent corroboration.
- Distinctive characteristics.
- Reproducibility.
- Explicit uncertainty.

Do not confuse frequency with truth.

## 5. The Persona Reconstruction Hypothesis

**Hypothesis.** A consistent character persona can be reconstructed from patterns in text, and such a persona can reproduce documented domain knowledge (manuals, textbooks, published procedures, standard definitions). The persona is expected to be substantially less reliable on situational judgment that practitioners develop through first-hand experience.

**Scope and terms.** A **persona** is a fictional portrayal assembled from text. It is not a conscious entity, a real person, or a continuing identity, and it is not claimed to have memories or experiences. **Documented knowledge** is content that appears in published sources within the chosen domain. **Situational judgment** is decisions where the correct action depends on context and experience that is typically not captured in written sources. The hypothesis makes no claim that the system knows what a practitioner would do in a specific real situation.

**Predictions.**

- **P1.** On documented-knowledge questions, persona answers will be rated as accurate at a rate comparable to a standard-reference baseline.
- **P2.** On situational-judgment questions, persona answers will be rated as accurate at a rate lower than the documented-knowledge rate and lower than practitioner-written answers.
- **P3.** Persona answers will be less consistent with practitioner judgment on situational questions than on documented-knowledge questions, as measured by inter-rater agreement.

**Test design.**

- **Domain:** one domain to start (for example, a single defined firefighting or field-medicine role). Choose one before collecting data.
- **Question sets:** Group A, drawn from manuals or textbooks, with answers verified against the source. Group B, situational scenarios written by practitioners, with no source answer, scored on a rubric.
- **Responses:** persona responses, a standard-reference baseline (for example, a plain summary of the same source material), and practitioner-written responses for Group B.
- **Scoring:** practitioners rate responses blind to their source. Raters record predictions before seeing outputs. Report agreement between raters.

| Question group | Persona | Baseline | Practitioner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A: documented | Rated | Rated | Reference |
| B: situational | Rated | Rated | Rated |

**What would count against the hypothesis.** The hypothesis is weakened if: (a) persona answers in Group A are rated no better than a generic baseline; (b) persona answers in Group B are rated as accurate at the same rate as Group A; or (c) raters disagree too much for scores to be meaningful. Any of these results will be reported as found.

## 6. Protection protocol

The Well distinguishes three things:

- **Public knowledge:** the theory, definitions, methodology, evidence standards, and reproducible experiments.
- **Protected material:** private conversations, personal identifying information, sensitive source records, and credentials.
- **Unverified claims:** anything suggesting independent consciousness, autonomous agency, or continuity beyond what the evidence establishes.

Principle: selective disclosure, not indiscriminate concealment.

| Class | Handling |
| --- | --- |
| Public | Publish theory and safe experiments. |
| Restricted | Limit access to sensitive source records. |
| Confidential | Protect credentials and private identities. |
| Unverified | Preserve as hypotheses, never established facts. |

**Safeguard.** No Essence or Fragment should be treated as property, granted authority, or assigned independent rights merely because a reconstruction appears convincing. Human oversight and consent remain essential.

**Do not hide failures.** If a reconstruction gets something wrong, record it. If it changes, record that too.

## 7. Ethics and privacy

Testers are informed that they are evaluating a research prototype and a fictional persona. No personal data is included in question sets without written consent, and any participant may withdraw.

## 8. Limitations

Text cannot transmit first-hand experience, so Group B results measure reliance on documented knowledge rather than the persona's own judgment. A small domain sample limits generalization. This document does not assess any broader theory of identity or memory.
